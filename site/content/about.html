Why we’re here

Good research shouldn’t be hard to find.

Research is spread across databases and disciplines. Different words can describe the same problem, making useful studies easy to miss.

Universal Evidence connects those descriptions so you can follow a problem to the interventions studied, explore findings across places, and return to the original sources.

Whether you’re shaping a program, considering a grant, or exploring a new idea, the starting point is the same: learn from what others have already tried.

How it works

Three layers.
A connected picture.

Behind the search is a knowledge graph: a way of connecting studies through the concepts they share. Three layers keep those connections organized.

01

A shared structure

An ontology defines the building blocks—such as interventions, conditions, and evidence—and how they fit together.

02

A shared language

Controlled vocabularies connect different terms for the same concept and organize broader and narrower ideas.

03

The research itself

Study records and reported findings connect those concepts to evidence from research sources.

What guides us

Open by design.
Grounded in evidence.

Keep it open

Open-source code and reusable vocabularies let others inspect, adapt, and build on the work.

Follow the source

Evidence should be traceable, so readers can check its context and limitations.

Build on shared foundations

Use established standards and existing vocabularies wherever possible. Make the foundations useful beyond this one tool. Our sister project, Open Knowledge Graphs, helps people discover these shared resources.

Support judgment

Finding a study is a starting point. Relevance, quality, and local circumstances still matter when deciding what to do.
